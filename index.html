01 — The Problem

The Efficiency Plateau.

Nearly every nonprofit now uses AI in some form. Almost none of them are getting much from it. One or two staff quietly draft with a chatbot; the organization has no shared workflows, no governance, and no roadmap — so the gains stay real but shallow, and the sector's own researchers have a name for it.

92%of nonprofits report using AI in some form
7%report major improvement in what they can accomplish
81%use AI individually, with no shared workflows
47%have no AI governance policy at all

Source: 2026 Nonprofit AI Adoption Report — Virtuous & Fundraising.AI (346-organization benchmark).

Small nonprofits and rural tourism businesses are not early — they are behind, and they know it.

06 — Why Us

A translator, not a vendor.

Enterprise consultancies bill by the hour and scope in six figures. Generic AI freelancers are affordable but cannot answer the domain questions — what a cooperating agreement permits, how donor stewardship actually works, why August staffing looks nothing like February's.

Clients are buying a translator who has run the programs, managed the seasonal payroll, written the grant reports, and built the AI systems.

The trust barrier is as large as the skills barrier. Leaders of conservation organizations are rightly cautious about donor data, mission integrity, and public perception — they will hire the person who demonstrably shares their values before they hire the person with the flashier portfolio.

In this sector, governance is not overhead — it is the product feature buyers are most anxious about.

10 — Questions

Asked and answered.

Enterprise firms price engagements out of reach for a small organization. Freelancers are affordable but do not understand seasonal operations, interpretation and education missions, donor stewardship, cooperative agreements with land agencies, or the booking and volunteer systems you actually run on. This practice sits at the intersection: conservation operations experience plus current, daily practice building working AI systems.

Donor and member data receive the strictest handling tier, and no client data enters a tool that has not passed a privacy review. Every system that gets deployed has human review points, a named owner, simple evaluation checks, and a rollback path. If something cannot be done safely, the recommendation will be not to do it.

A readiness assessment runs two to four weeks. An implementation sprint runs four to eight. A workshop is a single day. Fractional AI Director work is monthly with a six-month minimum, because organizational change does not happen in three.

Nearly half of nonprofits operate with no policy at all. That is a real risk, and it ships as part of the work rather than as an upsell.
